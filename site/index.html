Your menu bar, in drawers.

Smart Sort

A drawer for every kind of thing.

Smart Sort knows 244 menu bar apps by name. It finds out which app owns each icon, looks up its purpose and proposes the drawers in one click. New icons sort themselves.

By purpose

A drawer for each kind of app you run, six at most. Lone icons gather in Other.

By developer

One drawer per company, read from each app's signature: Google, Proton, Microsoft, each shown by its initial.

One drawer

Every icon in a single drawer, for the quietest menu bar.

What stays

The clock, Control Center, Battery, Wi-Fi and Sound stay in the menu bar, with anything you choose to keep.

New icons sort themselves

Install an app and its icon goes straight to its drawer. Or let new icons show, or hide them: your choice.

Drawers

Every icon, one click away.

Click a drawer and a glass panel drops under it, each app shown with its own icon and name. Click one and the icon's real menu opens.

Right under your click

A drawer drops as a small glass panel right under its icon, never a long menu off to the side. Click an app and its own menu opens there.

Every icon, when you ask

Option-click Tiroir's icon, or rest the pointer on an empty part of the menu bar, click it or scroll there: every icon shows, in the menu bar or in the All drawer, and hides again once you are done.

Focus

Press ⌃⌥⌘FControl Option Command F before a presentation or a screen share: everything but the clock and Control Center leaves. Press it again and it all comes back.

Keyboard first

In a drawer, arrow keys move, Return opens and typing filters. Every drawer, and any single icon, can have its own shortcut.

Move without dragging

Right-click an icon in a drawer to move it to another drawer, keep it in the menu bar or hide it.

Hidden, never lost

Icons you never need to see stay out of the menu bar and one search away, in the All drawer too.

Profiles and triggers

The right menu bar at the right moment.

Keep one setup for the laptop and one for the desk, and let Tiroir switch between them, show a drawer or turn on Focus while something is true.

A whole setup

The drawers, where each icon goes, and the appearance, under a name: one for the laptop alone, one for the desk, one for a talk.

Always up to date

While a profile is active, every change you make is saved into it: switch away and back, and it is as you left it.

Yours to arrange

Rename, duplicate, reorder or delete a profile; deleting the active one leaves your menu bar as it is.

When

An app is open or in front, the Mac runs on battery or below a level you choose, an external display is connected, it is between two times, the camera or the microphone is in use.

Then

Switch to a profile, show a drawer's icons or a single icon in the menu bar, or turn on Focus. Your own Focus or Show Every Icon always wins.

Nothing waits in the background

Each trigger listens for the one change it needs, and only while it is on. No polling, and no new permission.

Settings

The top right of your Mac, your way.

Layout draws your menu bar at its real size, the notch included, with the room left beside it. Appearance tints it, floats it or splits it in two.

At its real size

Your menu bar as Tiroir sees it, the notch included, with the room left beside it and the icons that would not fit.

Room for more icons

Tighten the space between icons on macOS 26, and let icons that stop fitting beside the notch move into a drawer by themselves.

Drag, or right-click

Move an icon between the menu bar, your drawers and Hidden by dragging it, or with a right-click that also opens it or gives it a shortcut.

Full width, floating or split

Tint the whole bar, float it with round ends, or split it in two pieces that leave the notch and the middle free.

A color or a gradient

With its strength, a hairline along the bottom and a soft shadow underneath.

A look for Dark Mode

Give Dark Mode a tint of its own: Tiroir follows your Mac as it switches.

An icon for each drawer

Each drawer shows a monochrome icon, drawn like the rest of your menu bar, with its name or its count if you like. A drawer can also show an emoji or a few letters, if you prefer.

A shortcut for everything

Search, the All drawer, Show Every Icon, Focus, each drawer, each profile, and any single icon, whose menu opens wherever it lives.

Tiroir's icons where you want them

Next to Control Center by default, its icon then your drawers in the order you set. Or place them yourself with a Command-drag.

In your language

English, French, German, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, Japanese, Korean and Chinese, following the language of your Mac.

For everyone

Every control has a name for VoiceOver, the keyboard reaches everything, and panels follow Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast.

Your setup in a file

Export your drawers, profiles and settings, and import them on another Mac. For a bug report, Copy Diagnostics gathers what helps, and sends nothing.

Your Mac

Two versions of macOS. Two honest engines.

macOS 27 rebuilt the menu bar, so Tiroir carries an engine for each version. It picks the right one at launch, and Settings tells you which one runs.

macOS 26 Tahoe

Icon by icon.

Tiroir keeps an invisible divider in your menu bar and moves icons behind it with Command-drags, as you would by hand. The pointer is hidden while icons move, then put back.

  • HidesAny icon, one at a time
  • Opening an iconIt steps beside its drawer while its menu is open, then back to its own place
  • The pointerHidden during a move
  • While icons are hiddenNothing else changes

macOS 27 Golden Gate Beta

App by app.

macOS 27 draws the whole menu bar itself, and apps can no longer move its icons. Tiroir asks macOS to show every app except those in drawers. It never moves the pointer.

  • HidesWhole apps: an app with two icons hides both
  • The pointerNever moves
  • While icons are hiddenmacOS 27 also hides Focus and the camera and microphone indicators
  • Where Tiroir runsThe Applications folder
Support for macOS 27 is in beta until it has run on many Macs. If anything looks wrong, Show Every Icon brings everything back in one click, and quitting Tiroir does too. An issue on GitHub helps fix it.

Privacy

It opens your icons. It never looks at your screen.

Tiroir needs to know which app owns each icon, and nothing else.

One permission

Accessibility, to see which app owns each icon and to open it for you. Never Screen Recording, never Input Monitoring.

No screenshots

Tiroir never takes a picture of your menu bar. Drawers show each app's own icon, read from the app itself.

No telemetry

No account, no analytics, no crash reports. The only connection is the update check, and you can turn it off.

Private functions in one file

Hiding the pointer on macOS 26 and the menu bar of macOS 27 need a few private functions. They all live in PrivateAPI.swift and are looked up at run time: if one disappears, only its feature turns off.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. No trial, no subscription, no account: Tiroir is free and open source under the MIT License.

Does it work on macOS 27?

Yes, in beta. macOS 27 draws the menu bar itself and only lets apps hide whole apps, so there an app with two icons hides both, and Tiroir never moves the pointer. While icons are hidden, macOS 27 also hides Focus and the camera and microphone indicators.

macOS 27 only lets apps in the Applications folder change the menu bar: Tiroir offers to move itself there.

Why does Tiroir need Accessibility?

To see which app owns each icon, and to open an icon's menu for you. macOS only shares both with apps you allow in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Accessibility. Tiroir asks for nothing else: never Screen Recording, never Input Monitoring.

Will it slow my Mac?

No. At rest Tiroir polls nothing: it wakes when an app starts or quits, when a display changes, or when you click, and a time trigger sets one alarm for its next change. Its memory at rest is in the Your Mac section above, and Settings, General shows the live figure.

What happens if Tiroir quits or crashes?

Every icon comes back. On macOS 26 the invisible divider that hides them leaves the menu bar with Tiroir; on macOS 27, macOS drops Tiroir's request as soon as Tiroir stops. On macOS 26 the order of your icons stays as Tiroir left it, as if you had arranged them yourself.

Can I hide Apple's icons?

On macOS 26, yes: Wi-Fi, Battery, Sound and the other Control Center icons can go into a drawer like any other. macOS keeps the clock and Control Center itself in place. On macOS 27, Apple's own icons cannot be hidden, so they stay in the menu bar.

Does it read my screen?

No. Tiroir never asks for Screen Recording and never takes a picture of your menu bar. Through Accessibility it reads each icon's app, position and label, and drawers show the apps' own icons, read from the apps themselves.

How is it different from Bartender or Ice?

Tiroir gathers icons into drawers, each shown in the menu bar as a single icon, proposes those drawers itself, and works on macOS 26 and 27 with Accessibility alone. Profiles, triggers, search, shapes and the space between icons are there too. It is free and MIT licensed. Bartender is paid and closed, and version 6 needs Screen Recording; Ice no longer works on macOS 27. The table above compares them line by line.

Can the menu bar change by itself?

Yes, with triggers. While an app is open or in front, the Mac runs on battery, an external display is connected, during the hours you choose or while the camera or the microphone is in use, Tiroir can switch to a profile, show a drawer or a single icon, or turn on Focus. When the condition ends, the menu bar goes back.

Is Tiroir in my language?

Tiroir speaks English, French, German, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, Japanese, Korean and Chinese, and follows the language of your Mac.

How do I uninstall it?

Right-click any drawer and choose Quit Tiroir: every icon comes back. Then move Tiroir to the Trash, or run brew uninstall --cask tiroir if you installed it with Homebrew. Its settings live in one preferences file, ch.rubencatalao.tiroir.

Can I suggest an app for Smart Sort?

Yes, please. Smart Sort starts from a catalog of 244 menu bar apps, kept in one JSON file in the repository. Add your app's bundle identifier and its drawer and open a pull request, or open an issue with the app's name.
